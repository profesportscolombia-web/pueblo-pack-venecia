Venecia, Antioquia

Audio walk + 2-day itinerary + map + survival Spanish. The coffee town at the foot of the world's tallest climbable natural pyramid.

Welcome

Venecia is a small coffee town about 58–60 km (roughly 2 hours by road) from Medellín. Around 12,500 people live in the municipality, and fewer than half of them in the town itself. Its landmark is Cerro Tusa, a pyramid-shaped mountain rising to 1,850 m.

Two days in Venecia

Day 1 · Town, coffee and murals

MorningBus from Medellín's Terminal del Sur (about 2 hours). Check in, leave your bags.
MiddayLunch: try menú del día (soup + main + drink) or the town's signature plate, La Montaña Sagrada.
AfternoonDo the audio walk (stops 1–8): main park, church, archaeology museum, coffee, murals, Casa de la Cultura.
Late afternoonCoffee tasting. Buy water and snacks for tomorrow: shops open late.
EveningDinner near the church atrium or the pedestrian street. Sit in the park like the locals do.
On weekends the main square can be loud until late. Ask for a quieter room when you book.

Day 2 · Cerro Tusa

EarlyHire a local guide (the municipality's tourism office can connect you). Move to the base by car or moto-taxi (15–20 min).
MorningClimb. The municipality lists 2 to 2.5 hours from the base to the summit; a local tourism site estimates 6–8 hours for the full outing, difficulty high.
MiddayBack in town: shower, lunch, ice cream or coffee.
AfternoonBus back to Medellín. Check the last departure the day before.
Access rules (to confirm): one 2026 travel blog reports that the park gate is open 7:00–11:00 a.m., that only 150 hikers are allowed per day, that the mountain is closed on Tuesdays, and that you must climb with a certified guide (about US$20 per person on weekends). Confirm with the town hall or the park operator before you plan your day.
Safety: the climb is steep and exposed. Go with a guide, check the weather, wear shoes with grip, carry water and a rain jacket, and do not go if you are afraid of heights or unwell. Follow the posted warnings in the main square.

Add-ons if you have a third day

  • Bolombolo, a river town on the Cauca River with a boardwalk (malecón) and a famous fish cake (torta de pescado).
  • Cerro Bravo and old pre-Hispanic paths: a longer hike.
  • Coffee farm experience: ask your hotel; several farms near town offer tours.
  • Local market: last Sunday of each month. Check before you go.

Getting there and costs

Bus from Medellín

Buses leave from the Terminal del Sur (South Bus Terminal). The ride is about 2 hours. Some travelers also catch it on the way near metro stations; at busy hours it can be full, so the terminal is safer.

What to eat

  • La Montaña Sagrada: coffee-sauce pork rounds, plantain purée served on a bijao leaf (shaped like the mountain), yuca fritters and farm cheese. Made with local ingredients.
  • Torta de pescado (fish cake), a local favourite.
  • Arepas grilled over charcoal.
  • Coffee of origin from nearby farms.
Many places close early in the evening. Eat dinner before 8 p.m. (2018 note; check current hours).

Pack list

Water, snacks, hat, sunscreen, rain jacket, shoes with grip, cash in small notes (card machines can fail), offline map, a charged phone and power bank.

Best time

The Cerro Tusa festival is at the end of June and beginning of July. Dry spells are better for the climb. Check the forecast.
